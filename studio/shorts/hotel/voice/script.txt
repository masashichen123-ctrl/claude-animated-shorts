In 1861, Chicago lifted a six-story hotel into the air, with people still sleeping in it.
Chicago was built on a swamp, and the only way to fit sewers under the streets was to raise the whole city.
The biggest job was the Tremont House, a brick hotel downtown.
A young man named George Pullman promised he could lift it without disturbing a single guest or breaking a single window.
He slid thousands of jackscrews under the building, with hundreds of men standing beside them.
On a signal, every man gave his screws a half turn. Then another. And another.
Inch by inch, the whole hotel rose.
Luckily, the guests didn't have to check out.
Unluckily, the front steps now led up to a hotel that kept moving.
You see, one jack can lift a car with a few turns of your wrist.
Thousands of them, all turned together, can lift a building, as long as no corner goes up faster than the others.
Six feet later, the Tremont House sat on the new street, without a single broken window.
And what did the man who could lift a hotel without waking anyone do next?
He built the sleeping car: a hotel room on wheels.
