# The Exploding Whale (1970) - script

## Facts (sources: Oregon Encyclopedia "Florence Whale Explosion"; OPB "Curious Northwest: Florence whale explosion"; KATU "Infamous tale of the exploding whale"; Mental Floss "Florence, Oregon exploding whale 1970"; Popular Science "exploding whale")
- November 1970: a 45-foot, 8-ton sperm whale washed ashore on the beach near Florence, on Oregon's south coast.
- The job of removing it fell to the Oregon Highway Division. Assistant district highway engineer George Thornton, after consulting Navy and munitions experts, decided to treat the carcass like a boulder and blow it up.
- About half a ton (20 cases) of dynamite was used, on 12 Nov 1970.
- The expectation was that the blast would break the whale into pieces small enough for scavengers like seagulls to clean up.
- KATU reporter Paul Linnman and photographer Doug Brazil filmed it, with a crowd of onlookers.
- Large chunks of blubber flew over the crowd; one crushed a car parked nearly a quarter mile away (the state reimbursed the owner).
- Most of the whale remained on the beach; the highway crew buried the rest.
- The beach area was later named Exploding Whale Memorial Park.

## Narration (Kokoro am_michael)
1. In 1970, Oregon tried to get rid of a dead whale with dynamite. It did not get rid of the whale.
2. A forty-five foot, eight-ton sperm whale had washed up on a beach near Florence.
3. Nobody knew how to move it, and it was starting to smell.
4. So the job went to the Highway Department.
5. Their engineer decided to treat it like a boulder, and blow it up with half a ton of dynamite.
6. The plan: blast the whale into tiny pieces, and let the seagulls eat the rest.
7. A crowd gathered on the dunes. A TV crew started filming.
8. Then they pushed the plunger.
9. Luckily, the whale exploded. Unluckily, it exploded on everyone.
10. Huge chunks of blubber rained down on the crowd. One crushed a car parked nearly a quarter mile away.
11. And most of the whale was still lying right there on the beach.
12. So the highway crew did the one thing they'd been trying to avoid. They buried it.
13. Today, that beach is a park named after the exploding whale.
