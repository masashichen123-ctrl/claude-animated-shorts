# The 1904 Olympic Marathon - script

## Facts (sources: Wikipedia "Athletics at the 1904 Summer Olympics - Men's marathon"; Smithsonian "The 1904 Olympic Marathon May Have Been the Strangest Ever"; Olympics.com "St. Louis marathon: the strangest race in Olympic history"; Olympedia results; Wikipedia "Fred Lorz")
- 30 Aug 1904, St. Louis Olympics. Hot (about 90°F) on dusty dirt roads; officials' cars and horses drove ahead, kicking up dust.
- Only two water stops on the course (a water tower and a well), per Smithsonian; the organiser wanted to study dehydration.
- Fred Lorz (USA) crossed the finish first, but he had quit around mile 9 and ridden in a car to about mile 19. He was disqualified, and he won the 1905 Boston Marathon.
- Félix Carvajal (Cuba) ate apples from an orchard that turned out rotten, got cramps, lay down for a nap, and still finished fourth.
- Len Taunyane was chased nearly a mile off course by dogs; he finished ninth.
- William Garcia collapsed from road dust and spent days in hospital.
- Thomas Hicks (USA), the official winner, was kept going by his trainers with strychnine (a rat poison), egg whites and brandy. He was near collapse and hallucinating, and was held up/carried at the finish.
- 32 starters, 14 finishers.

## Narration (Kokoro am_michael)
1. At the 1904 Olympics, the marathon winner was given rat poison. And that wasn't even the strangest part.
2. The race was in Saint Louis, in ninety-degree heat, on dusty dirt roads.
3. Cars drove ahead of the runners, kicking up huge clouds of dust.
4. And there were only two places to get water on the entire course.
5. The first man across the finish line was Fred Lorz. The crowd cheered. Then they found out he'd ridden ten miles of it in a car.
6. One runner from Cuba stopped to eat apples from an orchard. They were rotten. So he lay down and took a nap. He still finished fourth.
7. Another runner was chased a mile off course by dogs.
8. And the real winner, Thomas Hicks, was barely alive. His trainers kept him going with egg whites, brandy, and strychnine.
9. Luckily, he made it to the finish line. Unluckily, his trainers had to carry him across it.
10. Out of thirty-two runners, only fourteen finished.
11. And the man who rode in the car? The next year, he won the Boston Marathon. On foot.
