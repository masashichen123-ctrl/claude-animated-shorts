# Chicago Lifts a Hotel (1861) - script

## Facts (sources: Wikipedia "Raising of Chicago"; Wikipedia "Tremont House (Chicago)"; WTTW Chicago "raising Chicago"; Gizmodo "Chicago was raised more than 4 feet in the 1800s to build its sewers")
- Chicago was built on low, swampy ground by Lake Michigan; streets were muddy and drainage was poor; cholera and other epidemics broke out in the 1850s.
- From the late 1850s the city raised its street grade several feet to install sewers underneath, and buildings were jacked up to match.
- In 1861 the firm Ely, Smith & Pullman (including the young George Pullman) raised the Tremont House, a large brick hotel, about six feet, while it stayed open with guests inside.
- Accounts give roughly 5,000 jackscrews and about 500 men (other accounts give other numbers); the men turned their screws a little at a time on a signal.
- Accounts claim the work was done without breaking a pane of glass.
- Pullman later founded the Pullman Palace Car Company, famous for railway sleeping cars.

## Narration (Kokoro am_michael)
1. In 1861, Chicago lifted a giant brick hotel into the air. With the guests still inside.
2. Chicago was built on a swamp. The streets were rivers of mud, and the sewage had nowhere to go.
3. Diseases like cholera kept breaking out.
4. So the city made a wild decision. Raise the whole city several feet, and build the sewers underneath.
5. The streets were easy. But the buildings had to go up too.
6. The biggest one was the Tremont House, a huge brick hotel.
7. A young engineer named George Pullman and his partners took the job.
8. They put about five thousand jackscrews under the building, and hired hundreds of men.
9. On a signal, every man gave his screws a tiny turn. Then another. And another.
10. Inch by inch, the hotel rose six feet.
11. And the hotel never closed. Guests kept eating, sleeping, and checking in, while the building rose beneath them.
12. Reportedly, not a single window broke.
13. Pullman later got rich building railway sleeping cars. So people could sleep, while the world moved beneath them.
