In 1970, Oregon tried to get rid of a dead whale with half a ton of dynamite.
A forty-five foot sperm whale had washed up on the beach, and the smell was unbearable.
Nobody knew whose job a whale was, so it went to the highway department, because legally, the beach was a road.
The engineer in charge had a plan: blow it into pieces so small the seagulls would eat them.
A man in the crowd, who had handled explosives in the army, told him twenty sticks would do it.
The engineer used twenty cases.
Everyone backed up a quarter of a mile, and a TV crew started filming.
The whale went up in a giant red cloud, and then it started raining blubber.
Luckily, nobody was hurt.
Unluckily, one enormous chunk landed on a parked car and flattened the roof.
The seagulls, scared off by the bang, never came back for lunch.
And most of the whale was still lying exactly where it started.
You see, a blast pushes hardest on whatever is closest. Slap a bowl of jelly: a little flies everywhere, and the rest just sits there.
Today, Oregon buries its whales.
As for the flattened car, it belonged to the explosives expert.
He had just bought it, brand new, in a sale called "Get a Whale of a Deal".
