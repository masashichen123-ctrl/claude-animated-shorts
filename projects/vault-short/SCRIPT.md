# The Time Lock (1870s) - script

## Facts (sources: Invention & Technology "The Glory of American Locks"; my-time-machines.net "A brief history of the time lock industry" and "Sargent & Greenleaf"; Safeguard Safes "The origins of the S&G time lock"; Sargent & Greenleaf company info)
- In the late 1860s, a series of robberies in which bank managers were kidnapped showed that a strong vault was only as safe as the person who could open it.
- James Sargent (of Sargent & Greenleaf, Rochester NY) built the first time lock for a bank vault in 1873, using parts from locks and two eight-day kitchen clocks.
- He personally installed it on the vault of the First National Bank in Morrison, Illinois, on 26 May 1874.
- A time lock keeps the vault door shut until a set number of hours has passed, so a kidnapped bank employee could not open it in the middle of the night, even under force.
- Time locks soon became expected on bank vaults, and are still used today.
- (Not used: the specific anecdote of a banker's family held hostage; the sources describe the threat in general terms only.)

## Narration (Kokoro am_michael)
1. In the 1860s, bank robbers stopped trying to break into vaults. They just kidnapped the banker.
2. The plan was simple. Grab the manager, march him to the bank at night, and make him open the door.
3. A steel vault is only as safe as the man with the key.
4. And a scared banker will open anything.
5. Then a lock maker named James Sargent had a clever idea. Build a lock that even the banker can't open.
6. He built it out of parts from two kitchen clocks.
7. The clock kept the bolt locked, and it wouldn't release until a set hour. No matter who was asking.
8. In 1874, the first one went onto the vault door of a bank in Morrison, Illinois.
9. Luckily, kidnapping no longer worked. Unluckily for the banker, neither did he.
10. The robbers could threaten him, tie him up, and wait. The clock did not care.
11. Soon, every bank wanted one.
12. And today, banks still use time locks. The best security guard turned out to be a clock.
